# ADR-036 — Candidate identity in runner results

Status: accepted. The user delegated this decision ("do whatever recommended") rather than choosing an option themselves; recorded here so the provenance is visible and the choice can be revisited on its merits.

## Current design

`CandidateSchema` in `@humanize/domain` describes a candidate finding with `nodeId`, `category`, `severity`, `confidence`, `exactText`, `explanation`, `evidence`, `replacement` and `requiresVerification`. It carries no identity field.

`VerificationSchema.results[].candidateId` refers to a candidate by id, and `RunnerResult` carries both arrays in one envelope. The upstream specification separates reviewer and verifier invocations and requires verification to decide publication per candidate, but does not say how a verification result is bound to the candidate it judges.

## Problem and consequence

There is no defined way to match a verification result to its candidate. Independent validation in `validateRunnerResult` can currently only check that `candidateId` values are unique and do not outnumber the candidates; it cannot confirm that a suppression actually suppresses the finding it names.

The consequence is a publication correctness risk rather than a cosmetic gap. If a verifier suppresses one candidate and the orchestrator binds that verdict to a different one, a finding the verifier rejected could reach a pull request while a sound finding is dropped. This violates the intent of INV-013, since a model response would be schema valid yet still misapplied.

## Decision and alternatives

**Decided: option 2.** A candidate's identity is `domainDigest('candidate', candidate)`, recomputed by the control plane and never accepted from the submitter. The options considered:

1. Add a required `id` to `CandidateSchema`, assigned by the orchestrator before verification. Explicit, but the identity is then supplied by a party the control plane does not trust when the envelope arrives from a runner.
2. Derive identity deterministically as `domainDigest('candidate', candidate)`. The control plane can recompute it and reject any mismatch, so a runner cannot invent bindings. It makes every candidate field part of its identity, so a corrected explanation changes the id.
3. Bind by array position. Simplest, and the most fragile across serialization and partial submission.

Option 2 was chosen because identity is derived rather than asserted. The control plane recomputes every candidate's digest from the candidate itself, so a compromised or buggy runner cannot bind a suppression to a different finding than the verifier judged — option 1 would have accepted that binding on trust. It also reuses the versioned canonical digest already introduced for snapshots and results, so there is one identity mechanism rather than two.

The cost is accepted deliberately: because every field participates in the digest, editing a candidate changes its identity and invalidates any verdict attached to the previous version. That is the safe direction — a stale verdict detaches instead of silently following an edited finding — and a verifier's own corrections travel in `correctedExplanation` and `correctedReplacement`, which leave the candidate untouched.

## Implementation impact

`candidateDigest` is exported from `@humanize/domain`. `validateRunnerResult` now rejects any verdict naming an identity absent from the submitted candidates (`VERIFICATION_CANDIDATE_UNKNOWN`), replacing the weaker candidate-count bound, and still rejects repeated verdicts (`VERIFICATION_DUPLICATE`). P1-S13 and P1-S14 are unblocked. No schema field was added, so `CandidateSchema` and `RunnerResultSchema` are unchanged on the wire and no migration or protocol version change is required.

## Verification and approval evidence

Covered by `packages/domain/result-validation.test.ts`: a verdict naming an unknown identity is rejected, and a verdict that would otherwise follow an edited candidate detaches instead, proving a verdict cannot be rebound to a finding the verifier did not judge. `pnpm check` passed with 84 unit tests across 17 files on 2026-09-17.
