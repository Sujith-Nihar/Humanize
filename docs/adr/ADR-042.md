# ADR-042 — Separate reviewer and verifier confidence thresholds

Status: accepted. Measured across 35 labelled cases on two models; the safety claim is
established, the recall claim deliberately is not.

## Current design

`reviewNodes` used one threshold, `DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE` at 0.90, for two different decisions: it
discarded a reviewer candidate whose self-reported confidence fell below it, and it discarded a
verifier verdict whose confidence fell below it.

## Problem and consequence

An attribution measurement over the review gold set found that **every suppression on that corpus
was `below_confidence`**, and none was `verifier_suppressed`. Candidates were being discarded
before the verifier ever saw them.

The two numbers are not the same kind of thing. A reviewer's confidence is a model's self-report
about its own output, which the architecture already describes as a heuristic rather than a
calibrated probability. A verifier's confidence is a considered judgement made against supplied
evidence. Using one cut for both meant a weak self-report was doing the verifier's job with far
less information, and the verifier — the component designed to make exactly this decision, and
the one holding the evidence — was never consulted.

The same measurement also contradicted a standing assumption. The production plan records that
the model half "contributes almost nothing". On this corpus the model published 6 findings on
Bedrock Claude Haiku 4.5 and 14 on local `qwen3.5:4b`, against 9 and 6 from deterministic rules
respectively. The model contributes materially; the threshold was discarding its work.

## Decision and alternatives

Split them. `DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE` becomes **0.60** and governs which candidates reach the
verifier. `DEFAULT_VERIFICATION_CONFIDENCE` stays at **0.90** and governs what is published. The
reviewer proposes broadly; the verifier decides.

Lowering both was rejected: the verifier's judgement is the last gate before a pull request, and
nothing suggests it should be relaxed. Removing the reviewer threshold entirely was rejected
because it is still a cheap way to drop obvious noise before spending a verification call.

## Implementation impact

Suppressions on this corpus move from `below_confidence` to `verifier_suppressed`, which is the
intended shape: candidates are now rejected for a reason someone examined rather than for a
number. `ReviewOptions` gains `verificationThreshold` so both remain configurable.

## Verification and approval evidence

**Established: the change is safe.** Precision remained **100%** with **zero false positives**
across four runs — two models at each threshold — including the legitimate-promotional cases
written to bait a reviewer into commenting on sound writing.

**Not established: that recall improves.** A probe at the lower threshold recovered
`formulaic-opener-world`, but repeating the gate showed the same corpus moving between 3 and 4
missed positives on both models. A one-case effect is **within run-to-run variance** on 35 cases,
so the recall gain observed is not evidence of one. Establishing it needs a substantially larger
corpus, or repeated runs with a reported variance, and neither exists yet.

The change is therefore justified by its structure — the verifier makes the decision it was built
to make — and by precision holding, not by a measured recall gain.
