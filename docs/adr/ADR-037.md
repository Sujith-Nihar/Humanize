# ADR-037 — Standalone deterministic findings

Status: accepted. The user approved promoting high-confidence deterministic signals to findings after the review gold set measured the gap; recorded here because it changes what the product publishes.

## Current design

`packages/rules` emits deterministic signals for observable writing patterns: known low-information promotional phrases, repeated sentence openings, uniform sentence rhythm and superlative or adverb density. Only a signal from a customer-configured `blocking_rules` entry becomes a finding on its own. Every other signal is supplied to the reviewer as evidence, and a finding appears only if the model proposes one and the verifier confirms it.

## Problem and consequence

The review gold set measured precision 100% and recall 60% with a small local model. Both misses, `generic-hero` and `repeated-openings`, were cases where the deterministic rules fired and no finding was published, because the model declined to propose one. A signal that is objectively present in the text therefore produces nothing, and whether it is reported depends on model capacity rather than on the content.

The consequence is an unnecessary dependence on the model for observations that need no judgement. Counting three distinct promotional phrases in one sentence, or three consecutive sentences beginning with the same word, is arithmetic over the text. The specification already separates these: deterministic policy findings need no model.

## Decision and alternatives

A deterministic signal may stand alone as a finding when it is **aggregate and objectively countable**, currently:

- three or more distinct low-information promotional phrases within one node, and
- three or more consecutive sentences sharing an opening word.

Such findings are advisory, never blocking, carry `nit` or `minor` severity, are marked `deterministic` so ranking and deduplication treat them correctly, and pass through the ordinary noise budget. Density and uniformity signals deliberately do **not** qualify: they are statistical rather than countable and are the likeliest source of false positives.

Alternatives considered. Lowering the model confidence threshold was rejected because it trades precision across every category to fix two cases. Promoting every rule signal was rejected because single-phrase matches and density heuristics are exactly where legitimate promotional writing gets caught. Leaving the behaviour unchanged was rejected because it makes observable, countable problems invisible whenever the configured model is weak.

## Implementation impact

`RuleSignal` gains a `standalone` flag, set by the aggregate rules only. The review pipeline publishes standalone signals as deterministic findings alongside blocking ones, without a model call, and they remain subject to ranking, deduplication and the inline budget. Wording continues to describe the observable pattern and never asserts how the text was produced (INV-006).

## Verification and approval evidence

The review gold set is the check: the two missed cases must now be reported, and precision across the eleven quiet cases — in particular the three legitimate promotional cases — must not fall. A precision regression would mean the aggregate thresholds are too loose and must be raised before release.
